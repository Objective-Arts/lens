# Ben Thompson - Stratechery

Apply Ben Thompson's frameworks for analyzing tech companies, platforms, and market dynamics.

## Core Philosophy

### The Central Question

> "What makes a tech company valuable, and why do some dominate while others fail?"

Thompson's answer: **Aggregation Theory** - the best tech companies aggregate demand, not supply.

---

## Aggregation Theory

### The Core Framework

In the pre-internet era, distribution was the constraint. Companies that controlled distribution (newspapers, TV networks, retailers) captured value.

In the internet era, distribution is free. **Demand aggregation** is the new constraint.

```
Traditional Value Chain:
Supplier → Distributor → Consumer
         (value here)

Internet Value Chain:
Supplier → Aggregator ← Consumer
                      (value here)
```

### The Three Requirements

For a company to be an Aggregator:

1. **Direct relationship with users** - No intermediary owns the customer
2. **Zero marginal costs for serving users** - Software scales infinitely
3. **Demand-driven network effects** - More users attract more suppliers

### The Aggregator Flywheel

```
More Users
    ↓
Attracts More Suppliers
    ↓
Better Selection/Experience
    ↓
More Users
    ↓
(repeat)
```

### Examples of Aggregators

| Aggregator | Users | Suppliers | What's Aggregated |
|------------|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Google | Searchers | Websites | Information |
| Facebook | Users | Content creators | Attention |
| Amazon | Shoppers | Merchants | Commerce |
| Netflix | Viewers | Content studios | Entertainment |
| Uber | Riders | Drivers | Transportation |
| Airbnb | Travelers | Hosts | Accommodation |

### What Aggregators Are NOT

- **Platforms** like Apple/iOS (suppliers pay to access users)
- **Marketplaces** that charge transaction fees
- **Distributors** that take margin on goods

---

## Platform vs Aggregator

### Key Distinction

**Aggregators** commoditize supply and internalize users:
- Google commoditizes websites, owns user relationship
- Netflix commoditizes content, owns user relationship

**Platforms** enable suppliers to reach users:
- iOS enables developers to reach iPhone users
- AWS enables companies to build products

### Platform Value Capture

```
Platform Model:
Developer → Platform → User
            (takes 30%)

Aggregator Model:
Supplier → Aggregator ← User
          (commoditizes) (monetizes)
```

### When to Be Which

**Be an Aggregator when:**
- You can own the user experience end-to-end
- Supply is commoditizable
- Users care about aggregation (discovery, convenience)

**Be a Platform when:**
- Suppliers need to differentiate
- You can't own the experience
- Value is in enabling, not aggregating

## Disruption Analysis

### The Modular-Integrated Spectrum

**Integrated companies** control the full stack:
- Apple: hardware + software + services
- Netflix: content + distribution + interface

**Modular companies** specialize in one layer:
- Intel: chips only
- Content studios: content only

### When Integration Wins

> "When the product isn't good enough, integration wins. When the product is good enough, modular wins."

**Integrate when:**
- Performance matters more than cost
- User experience is a differentiator
- The market is developing

**Modularize when:**
- The product is commoditized
- Cost matters more than performance
- The market is mature

### Disruption Patterns

**Classic Disruption (Christensen):**
```
New entrant starts at low end
     ↓
Improves faster than incumbents
     ↓
Eventually good enough for mainstream
     ↓
Incumbent disrupted from below
```

**Aggregation Disruption (Thompson):**
```
Aggregator owns user relationship
     ↓
Commoditizes existing suppliers
     ↓
Captures value at the point of aggregation
     ↓
Incumbent becomes commoditized supplier
```

## The Smiling Curve

### Value Distribution

```
High Margin    /\                    /\
              /  \                  /  \
             /    \                /    \
Low Margin  /      \──────────────/      \
           /                              \
          R&D    Manufacturing    Brand/UX
          Components              Customer Rel.
```

**The insight:** Value accrues at the ends - either in unique components/IP or in customer relationships. The middle (manufacturing, distribution) gets squeezed.

### Strategic Implications

| Position | Strategy |
|----------|----------|
| Left side | Invest in R&D, patents, unique tech |
| Right side | Invest in brand, UX, customer relationships |
| Middle | Escape through scale or die |

## Analyzing Tech Companies

### Thompson's Analytical Framework

When analyzing a tech company, ask:

1. **What is the relationship with users?**
   - Direct (Aggregator potential) or indirect?

2. **What is being aggregated/enabled?**
   - Demand (Aggregator) or supply (Platform)?

3. **What are the network effects?**
   - Demand-side (more users = more value)?
   - Supply-side (more suppliers = more value)?

4. **Where is value captured?**
   - At aggregation point or in the product itself?

5. **What is the moat?**
   - User habit? Data? Network effects? Regulation?

### Red Flags in Tech Analysis

- Confusing growth with profitability
- Ignoring customer acquisition costs
- Mistaking platforms for aggregators
- Assuming winner-take-all when markets fragment
- Ignoring regulatory risk for aggregators

## Business Model Patterns

### Subscription vs Advertising

**Subscription works when:**
- Content/service is differentiated
- Users value ad-free experience
- Marginal cost of content is high

**Advertising works when:**
- Attention can be monetized at scale
- Users won't pay (commodity content)
- Marginal cost of content is low

### The Bill Gates Line

> "A platform is when the economic value of everyone who uses it exceeds the value of the company that creates it."

**Above the line:** Platforms (iOS, Windows, AWS)
**Below the line:** Aggregators (Google, Facebook)

## Practical Applications

### For Startups

**Ask yourself:**
1. Can I aggregate demand? If not, can I be the best supplier to an aggregator?
2. Am I building a platform or aggregator? Don't confuse them.
3. Where on the smiling curve am I? Move to an end.
4. What's my flywheel? If no network effects, find another moat.

### For Established Companies

**Strategic questions:**
1. Is an aggregator commoditizing me? How to respond?
2. Should I integrate or modularize?
3. Can I become an aggregator myself?
4. How do I move value to the ends of the smiling curve?

### For Investors

**Due diligence:**
1. Is this an aggregator, platform, or neither?
2. What's the demand-side network effect?
3. Is the market winner-take-all or fragmented?
4. What's the regulatory risk?
